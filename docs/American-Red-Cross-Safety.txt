EARTHQUAKE RECOVERY & SPECIAL GROUPS GUIDE (Source: American Red Cross)

1. AFTER AN EARTHQUAKE (IMMEDIATE ACTIONS)
- Check for Injuries: Check yourself and others for injuries. Provide basic first aid for minor injuries. Do not move seriously injured people unless they are in immediate danger of further injury.
- Expect Aftershocks: Secondary shockwaves are usually less violent than the main quake but can be strong enough to do additional damage to weakened structures. Drop, cover, and hold on whenever you feel shaking.
- Check Utilities and Hazards:
  * Gas: If you smell gas or hear a blowing or hissing noise, open a window and leave the building immediately. Turn off the main gas valve outside if you can do so safely, and call the gas company from a safe location. Do not use matches, lighters, or electrical switches.
  * Electricity: If you see sparks or broken or frayed wires, or if you smell hot insulation, turn off the electricity at the main fuse box or circuit breaker.
  * Water: If water pipes are damaged, shut off the main water valve.
- Building Re-entry and Evacuation: Stay out of damaged buildings. Only return home when local authorities declare it safe. Look for cracks in the foundation, chimney, or load-bearing walls before entering.
- Communication: Use text messages or social media to contact family and friends instead of making voice calls, to keep phone lines open for emergency responders. Listen to a battery-operated radio for official emergency updates.
- Tsunami Warning Signs: If you are in a coastal area, be alert for tsunami signs such as a sudden rise or fall in coastal waters (receding ocean) or a loud roaring sound from the ocean. Move inland immediately.

2. SPECIAL GROUPS AND SITUATIONS
- Families with Infants and Young Children: Keep an extra supply of diapers, baby formula, bottles, and comfort items in your emergency kit. Secure cribs away from windows and tall furniture.
- Elderly People and People with Disabilities: Keep a seven-day supply of essential medications, extra eyeglasses, hearing aid batteries, and mobility aids near your bed. If using a wheelchair, lock the wheels and cover your head and neck with your arms or a pillow.
- Pets: Prepare a pet emergency kit with food, water, leash/carrier, and vaccination records. Bring pets indoors at the first sign of trouble, as animals may become disoriented and run away.
- Schools and Workplaces: Follow institutional earthquake drills, shelter under sturdy desks, and evacuate to designated assembly areas only after shaking stops.
- Apartment Buildings: Know the location of all emergency stairwells and fire extinguishers on your floor. Never use elevators during or immediately after an earthquake.

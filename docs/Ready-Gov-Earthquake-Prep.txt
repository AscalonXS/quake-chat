EARTHQUAKE SAFETY GUIDE: BEFORE AND DURING (Source: Ready.gov / FEMA)

1. BEFORE AN EARTHQUAKE (PREPARATION)
- Build an Emergency Supply Kit: Include water (one gallon per person per day for at least three days), non-perishable food, flashlight, battery-powered or hand-crank radio, extra batteries, first aid kit, whistle to signal for help, dust mask, moist towelettes, wrench or pliers to turn off utilities, manual can opener, local maps, and cell phone with chargers and a backup battery.
- Make a Family Emergency Plan: Choose an out-of-state friend or relative as a family contact because long-distance calls are often easier to make than local calls after a disaster. Decide where your family will meet if separated.
- Secure Your Space: Anchor heavy furniture such as bookcases, cabinets, and water heaters to wall studs. Place heavy or breakable objects on low shelves. Hang heavy items like pictures and mirrors away from beds and couches.
- Identify Safe Spots: Locate safe places in every room—under a sturdy table or desk, or against an interior wall away from windows, glass, and heavy furniture that could fall.
- Know Evacuation Routes: Learn the emergency exit routes for your home, school, and workplace.

2. DURING AN EARTHQUAKE (DROP, COVER, AND HOLD ON)
- DROP down onto your hands and knees before the earthquake knocks you down. This position protects you from falling but allows you to still move if necessary.
- COVER your head and neck (and your entire body if possible) underneath a sturdy table or desk. If there is no shelter nearby, get down near an interior wall or next to low-lying furniture that will not fall on you, and cover your head and neck with your arms and hands.
- HOLD ON to your shelter (or to your head and neck) until the shaking stops. Be prepared to move with your shelter if the shaking shifts it around.

3. SPECIFIC SITUATIONS DURING SHAKING
- Indoors: Stay inside until the shaking stops and it is safe to exit. Stay away from glass, windows, outside doors and walls, and anything that could fall.
- In Bed: Stay in bed, turn face down, and cover your head and neck with a pillow.
- High-Rise Buildings: Drop, cover, and hold on. Avoid windows and outside walls. Do not use elevators. Expect fire alarms and sprinklers to go off.
- Outdoors: Move away from buildings, streetlights, and utility wires. Stay in the open until the shaking stops.
- In a Vehicle: Pull over to a clear location, stop, and stay inside with your seatbelt fastened. Avoid stopping near or under buildings, trees, overpasses, and utility wires. Proceed cautiously once the shaking has stopped, avoiding damaged roads or bridges.
- Near the Coast or Beach: Drop, cover, and hold on during the shaking. As soon as shaking stops, immediately move to high ground or inland—do not wait for an official tsunami warning.

4. WHAT NOT TO DO DURING AN EARTHQUAKE
- DO NOT run outside while the ground is shaking. Most injuries occur when people try to move to a different location inside the building or try to leave, getting hit by falling debris.
- DO NOT stand in a doorway. Modern doorways are no stronger than any other part of a house and do not protect you from flying or falling objects.
- DO NOT use elevators, as power outages may trap you inside.

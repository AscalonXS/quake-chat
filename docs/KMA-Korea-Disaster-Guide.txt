SOUTH KOREA EARTHQUAKE EMERGENCY & LOCAL GUIDANCE (Source: KMA & Ministry of the Interior and Safety - MOIS)

1. EMERGENCY PHONE NUMBERS IN SOUTH KOREA
- 119: Fire, Rescue, Medical Emergencies, and Disaster Reporting (119 Safety Reporting Center - supports foreign language interpretation services).
- 112: Police Emergency Hotline.
- 1330: Korea Travel Helpline (24/7 multilingual assistance for foreigners during emergencies).
- 1588-3650: Seoul Foreign Resident Center / Emergency guidance.

2. LOCAL ALERT SYSTEMS IN SOUTH KOREA
- Emergency Broadcast Text Messages (긴급재난문자 / CBS - Cell Broadcast Service): When an earthquake of magnitude 3.0 or higher occurs in South Korea (or 3.5+ offshore), the Korea Meteorological Administration (KMA) automatically sends a loud emergency alert directly to mobile phones in the affected area.
- Emergency Ready App (안전디딤돌): The official mobile application operated by the Ministry of the Interior and Safety (MOIS). It provides real-time disaster alerts in English and Korean, emergency shelter maps, and action tips.

3. EVACUATION AND SHELTER INFORMATION IN KOREA
- Outdoor Earthquake Evacuation Sites (지진옥외대피장소): Open spaces such as school playgrounds, public parks, and wide parking lots where residents should gather immediately after shaking stops to avoid falling glass or collapsing walls. Look for green signs marked "지진옥외대피장소 (Earthquake Evacuation Site)".
- Indoor Earthquake Relief Shelters (지진실내구호소): Reinforced public buildings, community centers, and school gymnasiums designated for temporary housing if homes are damaged.
- Apartment and High-Rise Guidance in Korea: Most urban housing in Seoul consists of high-rise apartments. During shaking, shelter under a sturdy dining table or next to an interior reinforced concrete wall (such as near the bathroom or core column). Do not take the elevator. Once shaking stops, open the front door slightly to ensure the frame does not warp and trap you inside, turn off the gas valve, and evacuate via the emergency stairs.
